What this PR is about

Problem

A picture that arrives on its side stays on its side. Bloom could crop a picture and move it, but it could not turn it, mirror it, or set an item on the page at an angle. An author whose camera saved a photograph sideways had to leave Bloom, turn the file in another program, and put it back.

What the PR does

  • Rotate right on the picture menu turns the picture a quarter turn clockwise. An ordinary picture turns as a box; a background picture, which fills its page area and cannot turn as a box, has the picture inside it turned and its area reshaped to match. The result is what the author would have had if the picture had arrived already turned: upright, at the same framing, with any crop kept. A picture told to fill the page still fills it, ends clipped rather than blank bands.
  • Flip horizontal and Flip vertical mirror the picture about the axes the user sees, whichever way the picture and its box have been turned.
  • A rotation handle turns a selected item to any angle: a knob on a short stem above its top edge, snapping to every 45 degrees within 14 degrees of one, with CTRL to turn snapping off. Past 135 degrees the knob hangs from the item's other edge, because the top edge then points down the screen and the knob would sit behind the panel of controls. An item whose outline comicaljs draws (a speech bubble, a caption, a rectangle or an ellipse) gets no handle, because that outline stays upright.
  • Dragging, resizing and cropping work on a turned item. Mouse movement is converted into the item's own coordinates, and the item is shifted once so that the edge opposite the dragged one stays where it looks. A turned item also stays put across page loads and through a whole crop drag, because every check now reads the item's own laid-out box rather than the upright box the browser reports around it. A click picks the right item, which comicaljs alone cannot do. The control frame turns with the item: each handle shows the cursor for the direction it really moves, each tooltip stays level, and the crop marks count both turns, so they mark the two sides that are really hidden.
  • A turned item's contents stay reachable. A CSS transform makes the item a stacking context, so the z-index its contents use to clear the comicaljs canvas no longer reaches past the item. The canvas then covers it and takes the pointer, and the browser gives it no :hover, which is why a turned video lost its play button. Bloom now marks the turned item the pointer is really inside, using the same rotation-aware hit test a click uses, and the CSS accepts that mark in place of :hover.
  • Undo puts back a Rotate right, a Flip, and a drag of the handle, one step each. Reset Image now clears the crop, the picture's turn and mirror, and a transparency chosen by hand. It does not straighten a turned box, because that angle belongs to the box as its size and position do. The picture menu is now three groups: which picture is in the box, how it sits in the box, and the rest.

Every angle and mirror lives in the item's own inline transform, saved in the book HTML, so it appears in BloomPlayer and in PDF output, where no Bloom JavaScript runs.

What changed this run

One commit of work, plus a merge of master.

The rotation knob no longer jumps at a quarter turn. It moved to the item's other edge at every angle past 90 degrees. At 90 degrees the knob points straight out to the side, where the panel of controls does not reach it, so the move bought nothing and took the knob out from under the pointer at the angle an author stops at most often. The switch now waits for 135 degrees, which is the first snap angle at which the panel really does cover the knob.

A video on a turned item has its buttons back. The cause is the stacking context a CSS transform creates: the comicaljs canvas covers the turned item, so the browser never gives the item :hover and never shows the play button, and a click on any of the three buttons goes to the canvas instead. Three routes were weighed. Lifting the turned item above the canvas would clip comic tails. Taking pointer events off the canvas would break comicaljs's own tail handles, whose listeners live on that canvas. The route taken changes neither: Bloom marks the turned item the pointer is inside, using the hit test a click already uses, and the CSS accepts that mark in place of :hover. Measured in your Bloom on a video turned to 315 degrees: the play button appears on hover, the click plays the video, and pause and replay both work while it plays.

One question: the knob still moves at the half turn

Drag the rotation knob all the way round to 180 degrees and let go. At the moment you release the button, the knob jumps from the bottom of the item to the top, out from under your pointer. That is the angle where the move is genuinely needed: at 180 degrees the item's own top edge points straight down the screen, and the knob would otherwise sit behind the panel of controls below the item, where you could not reach it to turn the item back.

The rule deliberately does not move the knob while a drag is running, so that it stays under the pointer. The jump happens anyway because Bloom clears the drag before it puts the control frame back in place, so by the time the rule runs the drag is already over. It is one line to make the mouse-up leave the knob where it was, but then the knob sits behind the panel until you select something else and come back, which is worse.

So the choice is which of the two you would rather have at 180 degrees: the knob moving out from under your finger as you let go, or the knob unreachable until you reselect. It only happens at the half turn, and only on the drag that arrives there.
