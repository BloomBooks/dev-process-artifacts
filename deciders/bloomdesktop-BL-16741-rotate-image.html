What this PR is about

Problem

A picture that arrives on its side stays on its side. Bloom could crop a picture and move it, but it could not rotate it, mirror it, or set an item on a page at an angle. An author whose camera saved a photograph sideways had to leave Bloom, rotate the file in another program, and put it back.

What the PR does

  • Rotate right on the picture menu rotates the picture 90 degrees clockwise. An ordinary picture rotates as a box. A background picture rotates inside its area, which is reshaped to match, keeping the crop. Neither acts on an empty placeholder.
  • Flip horizontal and Flip vertical mirror the picture about the axes the user sees, however it is rotated. A cropped picture mirrors its visible part in place.
  • A rotation handle rotates a selected item to any angle, snapping to 45-degree steps, with CTRL to turn snapping off. Speech bubbles and similar shapes, and videos, get no handle.
  • Dragging, resizing, cropping and typing work on a rotated item. Clicks reach the right item and the right text, handles show the right cursors, and a rotated background keeps its framing when the page changes size or its picture is replaced.
  • Undo puts back each Rotate right, Flip and handle drag. Reset Image clears crop, rotation, mirror and a hand-chosen transparency, but does not straighten a rotated box. The picture menu is regrouped, and Escape closes it.
  • Publishing writes a rotated or flipped picture into the published image upright and cropped to what the page shows, for BloomPUB, ePUB, upload and spreadsheet export. The AI Image Editor gets the same upright view. An item rotated with the handle keeps its rotation in the published HTML.
  • Resize and crop handles follow the pointer at any edit zoom. Before, they ran ahead of it when zoomed, rotated or not.

Every angle and mirror is saved in the book HTML.

Worth knowing

  • A book uploaded to Bloom Library and downloaded again has a rotated picture's rotation built into the picture itself, so Reset Image will not un-rotate it there. The upload step has always written cropped pictures this way.
  • Dragging to select text inside a rotated text box still moves the box instead.
  • Fit Space is not remembered: after a background is fitted and the page changes shape, Bloom refits it to fit inside the area, which can leave bands. That is not specific to rotation.

Background pictures zoom in further each time the page changes shape

A background picture set to cover its area (for example after Become Background) is refitted whenever the page changes shape: an Origami split, a new page size, or a change of orientation. The refit keeps the old zoom when that is enough to cover, and otherwise zooms in. It never zooms back out. So after a few changes (landscape, then portrait, then a split) the picture was about three times more zoomed in than it needs to be, and the author sees less of it. The live check found this on a rotated picture, but the same arithmetic runs for every background picture (ceScale in adjustBackgroundImageSizeToFit), and it is already on master.
