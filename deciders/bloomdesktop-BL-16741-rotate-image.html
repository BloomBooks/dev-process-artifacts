What this PR is about

Problem

A picture that arrives on its side stays on its side. Bloom could crop a picture and move it, but it could not turn it, mirror it, or set an item on the page at an angle. An author with a photograph the camera saved sideways had to leave Bloom, turn the file in another program, and put it back.

What the PR does

  • Rotate right on the picture menu, shortcut Ctrl+R, turns a picture a quarter turn clockwise. An ordinary picture item turns as a box. A background image, which fills its page area and cannot turn as a box, has the picture inside it turned and shrunk to fit; that drops the crop, because the cropped region described the picture as it was.
  • Flip, with Flip horizontal and Flip vertical, mirrors the picture. The two axes are the ones the user sees, whichever way the picture has been turned.
  • A rotation handle on the control frame, a knob on a short stem above the top edge, turns the selected item to any angle. The angle snaps to every multiple of 45 degrees within 14 degrees of one, and the CTRL key turns the snapping off. An item whose outline comicaljs draws (a speech bubble, a thought bubble, a caption, a rectangle or an ellipse) gets no handle, because that drawn outline stays upright.
  • Dragging, resizing and cropping work on a turned item: mouse movement is converted into the item's own coordinates, and the item is shifted so that the edge opposite the one being dragged stays where it looks. Clicking picks the right item too, which comicaljs alone cannot do, because it tests the un-turned box.
  • Undo puts back a Rotate right, a Flip, and a drag of the rotation handle: one step for each command, and one step for each drag.
  • Reset Image now clears the crop, the turn and the mirror, and a transparency the user chose by hand, and puts the transparency back to Auto. It does not straighten a turned box, because that angle belongs to the box, as its size and its position do. The picture menu is now in three groups with a line between them: which picture is in the box, how it sits in the box, and the rest.

Every angle and mirror lives in the item's own inline transform, which is saved in the book HTML. It therefore appears in BloomPlayer and in PDF output, where no Bloom JavaScript runs.

Worth knowing

  • The whole feature was driven by hand in a running Bloom over the debugging protocol: the menu items, the handle, the snapping, and each step of Rotate right on both a background image and a picture item.
  • Three modules have no unit tests yet: canvasElementRotation.ts, imageContentTransform.ts and getSnappedRotation. Question 4 asks whether to add them now.
  • A speech bubble, thought bubble, caption, rectangle or ellipse gets no rotation handle. That is a decision from earlier in this work, not an omission: comicaljs draws those outlines upright onto a shared layer, so a CSS rotation would move the box away from its own outline.

Rotate right: your report of a horizontal flip

Earlier in this work you said that Rotate right "seems to do the right thing for the 1st call, but then it just flips horizontally", and asked for 90 degrees on every call. I measured the running Bloom afterwards and could not reproduce that. Each click adds a quarter turn on both paths. On a background image the picture went rotate(90deg) scale(0.667, 0.667), then rotate(180deg), then rotate(270deg) scale(0.667, 0.667), then no transform at all. On a picture item the box went 90, 180, 270, then upright. So the code steps by 90 degrees, and the question is what you saw.

One thing can look like a flip and is expected: at 180 degrees a picture with no obvious top and bottom, or one that is nearly symmetrical, is hard to tell from its mirror image. Another possibility is that the Bloom you were watching had not yet picked up the change, since the second and third clicks came before I had finished editing.

Notes

The crop marks on a turned item

When a picture is cropped, the four side handles on the control frame show a small mark on the sides where the picture runs past the box, so the user can see which way the crop goes. The code that decides this compares the on-screen rectangles of the picture and of the item (updateCurrentlyCropped, line 947). For a turned item those rectangles are the boxes drawn around the turned shape, which are bigger than the item itself, so the mark can appear on a side that is not really cropped, or appear on all four sides of a picture with no crop at all.

Nothing else goes wrong: the crop itself, the handles and the dragging are correct, and for an item at 0 degrees the marks are exactly as they were. Only the small mark is affected, and only on a turned item.

Notes

The handle is clipped at the top of the page area

The rotation handle sits on a stem 20 pixels above the top edge of the item. When the item is at the very top of its page area there is no room for it, and the part that does not fit is cut off, so the user may see only a stub of the stem and no knob. To turn such an item the user must first move it down.

The corner handles for resizing behave the same way today, so this is not new behaviour in the page; it is new only for this handle, which is further out than they are.

Notes

Unit tests for the three new pieces

The undo work has tests. The three pieces underneath it do not: the code that reads and writes the angle of an item, the code that turns and mirrors a picture inside its box, and the snapping of an angle to the nearest multiple of 45 degrees. All three are pure functions over a DOM element, so they are cheap to test in the existing test runner, and they are exactly the code a later change is most likely to break without anyone noticing.
