What this PR is about

Problem

A picture that arrives on its side stays on its side. Bloom could crop a picture and move it, but it could not turn it, mirror it, or set an item on the page at an angle. An author with a photograph the camera saved sideways had to leave Bloom, turn the file in another program, and put it back.

What the PR does

  • Rotate right on the picture menu, shortcut Ctrl+R, turns a picture a quarter turn clockwise. An ordinary picture item turns as a box. A background image, which fills its page area and cannot turn as a box, has the picture inside it turned and its own box reshaped to match. The rule a turn follows is this: you get what you would have had if the picture had arrived already turned. So the whole picture ends upright, it fills the page area where the turned picture is that shape, and any crop you had made is kept at the same framing. A picture you had told to fill the page keeps filling it, with its ends cut off rather than blank bands.
  • Flip, with Flip horizontal and Flip vertical, mirrors the picture. The two axes are the ones the user sees, whichever way the picture and its box have been turned.
  • A rotation handle on the control frame, a knob on a short stem above the top edge, turns the selected item to any angle. The angle snaps to every multiple of 45 degrees within 14 degrees of one, and the CTRL key turns the snapping off. An item whose outline comicaljs draws (a speech bubble, a thought bubble, a caption, a rectangle or an ellipse) gets no handle, because that drawn outline stays upright.
  • Dragging, resizing and cropping work on a turned item: mouse movement is converted into the item's own coordinates, and the item is shifted so that the edge opposite the one being dragged stays where it looks. Clicking picks the right item too, which comicaljs alone cannot do, because it tests the un-turned box. The mark that shows which side of a picture is cropped counts the turn of the picture as well as the turn of the box, so it marks the two sides that are really hidden rather than all four.
  • Undo puts back a Rotate right, a Flip, and a drag of the rotation handle: one step for each command, and one step for each drag. A turn moves the picture's box as well as the picture, and Undo puts both back.
  • Reset Image now clears the crop, the turn and the mirror, and a transparency the user chose by hand, and puts the transparency back to Auto. It does not straighten a turned box, because that angle belongs to the box, as its size and its position do. The picture menu is now in three groups with a line between them: which picture is in the box, how it sits in the box, and the rest.

Every angle and mirror lives in the item's own inline transform, which is saved in the book HTML. It therefore appears in BloomPlayer and in PDF output, where no Bloom JavaScript runs.

Bug: cropping does not work on a turned picture

This is a defect, not a rough edge. Turn a page background picture with Rotate right, then try to crop it by dragging the handle in the middle of one of its sides. The crop does not follow the pointer. Drag the bottom edge upward and the picture slides sideways instead. Drag a side handle and the picture moves up or down. There is no way to crop a turned picture by dragging, and no message tells the author why.

The cause: the code that moves the picture during a crop drag writes the new position in the directions of the box, and it does not know that the picture inside the box is lying on its side, so every movement comes out at ninety degrees to the one the author asked for. (continueSideDrag)

Two things soften it. Nothing is lost: the picture is all still there, and Undo or Reset Image brings it back, so the author loses the attempt and not the work. And it is older than this branch, so this PR did not break it: the drag code has never known about a turn of the picture, and the rotation handle could already produce the pair. What this PR changes is how easy it is to reach. Until now a turn threw the crop away, so crop-then-turn ended with no crop to drag. A turn now keeps the crop, so an author reaches the broken case in two clicks and will meet it. That is why it is on this report rather than left unsaid.

I did not fix it, because the fix is a second piece of coordinate work of about the same size as this whole commit, and it runs for every crop drag in Bloom, turned or not. The small marks that say which side is cropped were fixed here, because my change would otherwise have broken those as well.

Notes

Undo of a turn works only while the turned item is selected

Turn an item, then click on something else, then press Ctrl+Z: the turn is not undone. Select the turned item again and Ctrl+Z does undo it. Devin raised this and it is the one finding of its that is still open; it has been waiting for you since the run before last. (the thread, canUndoImageOperation, line 184)

The rule is deliberate in one sense: it copies the rule the older picture undo records already follow, where the selection has to be on the picture the record belongs to. Bloom keeps one undo stack for each item rather than one for the page, so an undo whose item is not selected is skipped rather than run. Whether that is right for a turn is a fair question: a user who turns a picture, clicks away, and then presses Ctrl+Z is told nothing about why nothing happened.
