What this PR is about

Problem

Many of the fix-ups a page needs happen only when that page is opened in the Edit tab: converting an old-style image to the 6.3+ background canvas element, recording each image slot's share of its page, canvas-element geometry. They need a real browser that has laid the page out, so nothing else applies them, and a book carries them on the pages someone happened to visit and nowhere else.

That is what BL-16852 reported. The AI image editor works across the whole book and sizes each image from the share-of-page its slot records — data only those fix-ups write. On an old dictionary it found a fraction of the images, and different runs found different subsets.

What the PR does

It gives Bloom a way to know whether a book has been through those per-page fix-ups, and runs them over the whole book, off-screen, when something actually depends on them.

  • Detection. A new kBrowserMaintenanceLevel, beside the existing kMaintenanceLevel and kMediaMaintenanceLevel, records how far a book has been taken. ProcessBook stamps that level and the page size it ran at into the book's HTML; the pass is due when the book records no level, a lower one, or a different page size. Deliberately not a Bloom-version comparison: version numbers are not comparable across channels, and keying off them would reprocess every book on every build.
  • A level from the future comes back down. A book a newer Bloom took past what we produce cannot go on claiming that level once we write it, because the pages we add would never get that Bloom's extra fix-ups. Saving brings the record down to ours, so the newer Bloom sees the book as due again. One-way — a level at or below ours is left alone. Because nearly every edit takes the fast single-page save, which rewrites one page and not the head, a book still recording a higher level is pushed onto the full-save path until the level is ours.
  • Where, in 6.5. Launching the AI image editor, and changing the page size. Both empty the editor first and put the page back afterwards: ProcessBook rewrites the book from a worker thread and must have it to itself, and every page it loads off-screen announces itself as loaded — including one carrying the id a live editor would be waiting on. Entering the Edit or Publish tab deliberately does not trigger it; that is held for 6.6 under BL-16877.
  • Strings. One translate="no" entry in BloomMediumPriority.xlf.

Team Collection books that are not checked out are skipped, since they cannot be saved.

Worth knowing

  • The page-size path was exercised by hand three times, in both directions: the pass ran, stamped the level and the new layout, and returned the editor to the same page rendering at the new size (671×952 for A5, 952×1347 for A4). The level clamp was verified separately — a book hand-stamped at 99 came down to 1 on selection.
  • Two paths are reasoned but not exercised: the restore-on-failed-write in BookStorage.Save (making the file replacement fail risks a dialog, so it is not something to provoke in CI) and the first-page fallback when the page you left no longer exists.
  • Pre-existing, not this change: a book on the legacy 5.6 appearance theme can be switched to Ebook 7x5, Ebook 2x3 or 13cm Square, which that stylesheet does not define, and every page then renders with zero height. It looks alarming while testing the page-size trigger and is unrelated; switching back restores it.
