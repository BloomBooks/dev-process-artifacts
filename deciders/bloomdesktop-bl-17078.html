What this PR is about

Problem

Dragging a corner handle to enlarge an inline image behaved differently by page orientation. In a portrait book the image stopped a little short of the edge of the text. In a landscape book it could be stretched past the bottom of the page.

Cause

A corner resize was limited only by the image's width (95% of the text block's width), and the height just followed from the picture's shape. A tall, narrow portrait block reaches that width limit first. A wide, short landscape block lets the image get taller than the block long before it gets that wide.

What the PR does

  • A corner resize now also stops where the bottom of the picture, plus its bottom margin, reaches the lowest point it can go without pushing text out of the block (getImageBottomLimitViewportPx, computeInlineImageMaxWidthPercentForBlockHeight).
  • In a block that shows one language, that point is the bottom of the text box. In a block that shows two or three, only the first shows the picture and the languages share the spare room; the picture may take all of it but leaves the other languages room for their text.
  • The picture also stops before it pushes the text that follows it out of the block, below a middle-docked picture or beside a floating one. This is the rule a drag already follows (continueResize).
  • An image that already reaches lower than that can be made smaller, but not bigger. Portrait pages behave as before.

Worth knowing

  • Nobody has tried this in a running Bloom yet. The browser tests drive the real resize gesture against real layout, but with a stand-in for the book's stylesheet rather than a real page.
  • When a width would push text out, each pointer move measures the layout up to eight more times to find the largest width that fits. Worth a glance for sluggishness on a slow machine.
