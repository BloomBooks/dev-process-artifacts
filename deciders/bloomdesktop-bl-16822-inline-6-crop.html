What this PR is about

Problem

An inline picture (a picture inside a text block, with the text wrapping around it) could be moved, resized and flipped, but not cropped. Every other picture in Bloom can be cropped, so inline pictures must be too, and cropping should work the way it does for a picture on a canvas. The inline crop had also grown its own copy of the canvas crop arithmetic and handle styling, which John asked to have shared.

What the PR does

  • Handles, as on a canvas. A bar in the middle of each side crops that side; the corner handles still resize; while the picture is cropped, a circle in the middle moves it inside its crop. A cropped side's bar is filled.
  • Flip, Reset Image, a new picture. Flip keeps the same part in view, mirrored. Reset Image removes the crop with the flip. Choosing a new picture removes the crop.
  • Stored on the wrapper, as a share of the picture. Four custom properties give the percentage cut from each side, so the crop holds at any page size and reaches every language's copy and every undo entry with no extra code. The text wraps around the part that shows.
  • Drawn by the book CSS, in every browser. The img's scale and translate place the whole picture, and a clip-path on the wrapper hides the rest; no object-view-box, which Firefox and Safari lack.
  • One copy of the crop code. imageCrop.ts holds the arithmetic both kinds of picture use: cropOneSide for a side handle and mirrorCropPosition for Flip, plus the canvas functions that moved there. The handle styling is shared as mixins in cropHandles.less.
  • Edges the dock holds. Cropping the top keeps the rest of the picture still. On the edge a dock holds, the picture slides toward that edge, as John chose.
