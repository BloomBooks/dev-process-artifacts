What this PR is about

Purpose

When a table's row or column was resized, the red overflow marks on its text changed about a second after the mouse was released, and they could be wrong in between. Text that did not fit a cell whose row had been dragged short hung over the row below. And text that fit its cell was marked anyway once a row was dragged taller than the table's own box. This PR makes the marks follow a table as it changes, and makes overflow in a table cell behave like overflow in a split-pane text box.

What the PR does

  • The overflow marks of a table's text update on every frame while a row or column boundary is dragged, and right after every table operation (Grow, Hug, adding or removing rows and columns, undo).
  • Text in a table only has to fit its own cell. Neither the table's box nor the canvas element it fills counts, since a row dragged taller runs past both; the boxes above them still do, up to the margin box, including the outer table's cell that holds a nested table.
  • Text that does not fit its cell is cut off at the cell's content edge instead of hanging over the row below, and the cell is marked red.
  • The overflow check of a table is fast enough to run on every frame: for a table of 34 text boxes it takes about 40 ms on a fast computer, and about 150 ms with the processor slowed to a quarter of its speed. It runs the page-wide part once for all the table's text boxes, measures their heights with one layout of the page, measures every box before marking any, and reuses descent measurements of text whose font and width have not changed.
  • The page's scroll bars are rebuilt only when a box's text starts or stops fitting, and each later check postpones a rebuild that is waiting, so it runs once a drag pauses. Before, every box's check asked for a rebuild every time: on a page with a big table that takes seconds on a slow computer, which froze a drag on every frame, and it ran after every pause in typing on every page.

Worth knowing

  • Measured in a running Bloom, on a 5 by 6 table: with the processor slowed to a quarter, a row drag rebuilt the scroll bars only after the mouse was released. At full speed, one rebuild ran during the drag after the row had reached its smallest height and stopped changing size, which is the same as a pause.
  • If you hold a row edge still for more than 200 ms mid-drag just after a cell's text has crossed its edge, the scroll bars are rebuilt then. Pauses at other times no longer rebuild them.
  • A save that starts during a row or column drag waits until the drag pauses, for at most 4 seconds.
  • Harvester: until harvester-production carries this change, BloomPUBs and ePUBs made by Bloom Library still draw text that is too big for a cell over the row below.
