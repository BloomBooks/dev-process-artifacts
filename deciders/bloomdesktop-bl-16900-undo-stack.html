What this PR is about

Problem

Bloom has five separate undo mechanisms — Change Layout mode's stack, the reader tools' per-box text undo, the picture-operation undo, CKEditor's per-box undo manager, and the browser's own — with no shared record of what happened in which order. The Undo button arbitrated between four of them with a hand-written chain of checks that also had to remember the BL-16558 rule (repaint the reader highlights after any undo that rewrites a text box), and there was no way to add a new kind of undoable operation (deleting a page, deleting a canvas element) without growing that chain. This is Stage 1 of retiring CKEditor and unifying Undo; its whole point is to change no user-visible behaviour.

What the PR does

  • Adds the one undo stack (bookEdit/undo/): index-based with truncate-on-push, O(1) canUndo/canRedo (C# polls canUndo on a timer), page scoping, lazy redo capture, and runUndoable for one-gesture-one-entry nesting. Entries are data, not page-frame closures, because the page iframe dies on every navigation.
  • Wraps the four existing mechanisms as legacy providers, consulted in exactly the order the old if-chain used, including the BL-16558 markup update after the reader-tools and CKEditor undos. handleUndo() and canUndo() become one-line delegations.
  • Tells the stack about page-frame navigation from the one place C# navigates it, using the .bloom-page element's id as page identity, so a previous page's entries are never undone onto the current one.
  • Binds Ctrl+Y in the page frame as a last resort: it acts only when nothing earlier claimed the keystroke and the stack has something to redo, so origami's and the reader tools' own Ctrl+Y and CKEditor's redo keep winning until they are converted. No Redo button, no C# involvement.
  • Adds live-check harnesses that attribute each undo gesture to the mechanism that ran, the paste/drop baseline they captured, and the project's progress log; the plan's §5 is rewritten for the topology now in force (short stage branches straight off master).

Verified in a running Bloom on 2026-09-07: the Undo button reaches the reader-tools undo, CKEditor's undo, origami's undo and the image undo exactly once each, and Ctrl+Y never double-fires. Tracked by card BL-16900, a subtask of BL-6681. Supersedes PR #8317.
