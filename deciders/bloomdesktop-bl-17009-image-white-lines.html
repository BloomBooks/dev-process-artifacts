What this PR is about

Problem

When a picture is meant to fill its area, a thin white line often shows along one or more of its edges. It happens most after dragging the origami splitter until the picture looks like it fills its pane, and with Fit Space or Expand to Fill Space. It is most noticeable where the picture runs to the edge of the page.

Cause

Bloom fits a background picture inside its picture area without cropping. Origami split positions are percentages, so the area is almost never exactly the picture's shape, and a sliver of 0.02 to 0.3px is left over. Fit Space had the same problem because it sized to whole pixels. Chrome also snaps an image to whole pixels before applying the Edit tab's zoom, so the drawn picture can stop up to another half pixel short. Either way, the white page shows through.

What the PR does

  • A background picture now overhangs each edge of its picture area that it reaches, or comes within 2px of reaching, by 1px. The area already clips it, so the overhang is never seen. This holds in both fit modes. The picture keeps its proportions, and a cropped picture keeps its crop.
  • Pages fitted before this change re-fit once when they are opened.
  • Fit Space counts as already done when the picture covers the whole area, is centred, and is enlarged no more than the overhang explains.
  • When the origami splitter resizes a pane, overlays on the picture keep their place. The repositioning code works out the size the picture was fitted to from its size and centred position.

Worth knowing

  • Not fixed, by agreement: at some zoom levels a light pixel column can still show exactly at the page edge where a picture runs off the page. Chrome anti-aliases the white page background and the clipped picture separately at a page edge that falls mid-pixel. The tester notes say so.
