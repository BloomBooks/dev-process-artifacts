What this PR is about

Problem

When Bloom makes a line drawing's white background transparent (on a coloured cover or page, for pictures its line-art check accepts, or whenever Background > Transparent is chosen), light grays in the drawing come out tinted by the background: the gray shading on the BL-16778 book looked pink on a pink cover and pastel aqua on an aqua one.

Cause

Since June 2026, RemoveWhiteBackground has made pixels with brightness 220 or more fully clear, kept 180 or less solid, and given the ones in between partial opacity while keeping their gray colour. A half-transparent gray over a colour is a blend of the two, so light shading turned into a pastel of the background.

What the PR does

  • RemoveWhiteBackground now treats each pixel as ink laid over white paper, as briefly written in May 2026: its opacity comes from its darkest colour channel, and its colour becomes the ink that gives back the original pixel when laid over white. On white a picture is unchanged; on a coloured background a light gray becomes a lighter shade of that colour.
  • Adds the comparison used to choose this: an explicit test that runs original-6.5 (the rule 6.5 uses today), proposed-6.5 (this PR), earlier rules from git history and ideas from the card over 25 pictures, and docs/transparency-report/, which builds a page showing every result over many background colours, with a timeline of which Bloom version used which rule.
