What this PR is about

Problem

When Bloom makes a line drawing's white background transparent (on a coloured cover or page, for pictures its line-art check accepts, or whenever Background > Transparent is chosen), light grays in the drawing come out tinted by the background: the gray shading on the BL-16778 book looked pink on a pink cover and pastel aqua on an aqua one.

Cause

Since June 2026, RemoveWhiteBackground has made pixels with brightness 220 or more fully clear, kept 180 or less solid, and given the ones in between partial opacity while keeping their gray colour. A half-transparent gray over a colour is a blend of the two, so light shading turned into a pastel of the background.

What the PR does

  • RemoveWhiteBackground now treats each pixel as ink over the picture's paper: its opacity comes from its darkest colour channel, and its colour becomes the ink that gives back the original pixel over white. On white a picture is unchanged; on a coloured background a light gray becomes a lighter shade of that colour.
  • A scanned drawing's paper is gray with grain. FindPaperBrightening takes the most common darkest-channel value from 215 up as the paper; when it is below 250, every channel is brightened by the same factor until the paper and grain within 10 of it are white.
  • Adds the comparison used to choose this: "Before 6.5", "6.5 beta initial", "6.5 Final" (this PR) and earlier rules, over 32 pictures including a BloomBridge scan and six synthetic pictures drawn to trip up the paper search, shown over many background colours with a timeline.
