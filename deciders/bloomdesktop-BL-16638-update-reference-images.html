The Local-Community back cover now reads at lower contrast

Bloom books can be published under a "branding", which controls the logos and boilerplate text on the cover pages. One of them is called Local-Community. The recent QR-badge work (BL-16370) redesigned what that branding puts on a book's back cover: the logo changed from the Bloom tulip above the word "Local" to a gift-box icon above "Bloom Local", and the paragraph explaining the subscription was rewritten.

Along with that rewrite, the paragraph lost the white box it used to sit in. On a book whose back cover is a light colour that is unremarkable. But on the 16x9 landscape book, whose back cover is dark grey, the text and logo now sit directly on that dark background — and "Bloom Local" plus its paragraph are noticeably harder to read than they were inside the white box. Someone looking at a printed or on-screen back cover would see grey-on-grey text where they used to see black-on-white.

This is BL-16370's shipped design, faithfully captured by the new baselines — it is not something this branch introduced or could fix. The question is only whether it is worth raising as its own piece of work. It may well be intentional, or judged good enough.

What should happen about it?

The visual regression suite hides all but the first failure in each case

The visual regression suite renders each test book and compares the result against stored screenshots. Each test case does this several times over: once for the whole-book preview, then once for every page as bloom-player shows it — up to eight comparisons in a single case.

Every one of those comparisons throws immediately when it fails. Because they run in sequence inside one test, the first stale screenshot in a case stops that case dead, and none of the later comparisons ever run — the screenshots they would have compared are not even captured.

That is what made this ticket twice the size it appeared. The preview screenshots were stale, so every case died at its first comparison, so no bloom-player page was ever compared — not by any nightly since BL-16370 landed, weeks ago. Accepting the 10 preview baselines revealed 10 player failures; accepting those would have revealed 2 more behind them. Left alone, this repeats on the next rendering change: the nightly will report one failure per case and stay silent about everything behind it, and each round of fixing costs a full run to discover the next layer.

The fix is to let a case run all of its comparisons, collect the failures, and fail once at the end with the full list — so one run tells you everything that is stale. The relevant code is the per-page loop in index.spec.ts:426 and the assertion at index.spec.ts:486.
